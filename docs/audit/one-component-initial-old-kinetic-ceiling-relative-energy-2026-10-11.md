# Initial relative energy from old physical kinetic bounds

Base: `cac8db71` (`derive initial uniqueness from bounded measurable energy`).

The initial bounded-measurable relative energy contract asked for time
measurability and a finite uniform bound of the *old-minus-selected*
physical `L²` Hilbert-square. Here those are derived from separate physical
velocity controls, without postulating a corresponding difference energy
bound.

* The selected canonical mild path is strongly continuous in the real
  physical `L²` Hilbert space through the existing weighted spectral decoder.
* Its norm is bounded by `6 * A`: three coordinates, Fourier isometry,
  the real spectral decoder bound, and the selected Picard norm `2 * A`.
* The old velocity is explicitly required to be a.e.-strongly measurable and
  uniformly bounded in physical `L²` on `(0,b)` by `B`.
* The energy of the old-minus-selected difference is then a.e.-strongly
  measurable and bounded by `(B + 6*A)^2`.
* This provides the measurable bounded-energy contract already known to
  imply initial physical compact-test equality and initial `L²`/`H³`
  right-trace conclusions; the full-time transverse theorem remains
  conditional on a strong-H³ actual-vorticity terminal endpoint.

The original old-velocity time measurability and uniform kinetic ceiling,
plus the epsilon-accurate PDE energy comparison and shrinking residual mass,
remain **unproved input hypotheses**. This step does not solve Navier--Stokes
regularity or weak--strong uniqueness from the bare open-time path.
