# Kinetic-anchored genuinely negative full-third H³ channel (244)

Base: `524d504c` — identify exact H3 lower transport slack and joint signed spectral growth.

The earlier necessary obstruction to nonextension was an unbounded *excess*
containing the positive `24 C₁ sqrt(E)` lower-transport allowance.  It did not
imply that either signed spectral cubic was itself negative.

The previously formalized kinetic-anchored physical absorption theorem
`h3PathCanonical_signedTopDominatesDissipation_on_every_subtail` has much
stronger content.  Given a genuine H³ energy-class path, kinetic anchor
`b ∈ (a,T)`, and hypothetical nonextension, every `d ∈ (b,T)` and `M ≥ 0`
has an actual physical time `t ∈ (d,T)` where

    2 M E(t) + D(t) < -T₃(t).

The completed physical-to-spectral correspondence gives, *at that same actual
Navier–Stokes time*, `T₃(t) = G₃(U(t)) + 3 M₃(U(t))`.  Consequently

    G₃(U(t))  + M E(t) + D(t)/2 < 0
       OR
    3 M₃(U(t)) + M E(t) + D(t)/2 < 0.

The finite cofinal-selection theorem fixes one of these two blocks *before*
choosing the later subtail or the threshold.  Normalizing by `E(t) >= 1`
yields, in a fixed channel, an arbitrarily negative value of

    (G₃ + D/2)/E          or          (3 M₃ + D/2)/E.

This upgrade is a real PDE-coupled sign statement: not a large excess caused
by a positive envelope.  It also retains one half of **full physical viscous
dissipation** in the selected channel rather than discarding viscosity.

A conditional continuation criterion follows: finite normalized lower floors
for both dissipation-adjusted signed cubics on one genuine terminal subtail
exclude the necessary obstruction.

These statements **do not** prove any unconditional signed coercive bound,
navier--Stokes regularity, or the existence of a singular solution.  The
established static Schwartz concentration counterexample remains an obstacle
for estimates that ignore the actual PDE dynamics.
